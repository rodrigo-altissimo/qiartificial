# Headline Testing Set Quality Checklist

> Source: DotCom Secrets + Traffic Secrets - Hook Frameworks
> Version: 1.0
> Use: When creating and evaluating headline variations for testing

---

## Pre-Requisites
- [ ] Target audience pain points and desires mapped
- [ ] Core offer/benefit clearly defined
- [ ] Competitor headlines analyzed (5-10 competitors)
- [ ] Understanding of headline formulas and frameworks

---

## STEP 1: Headline Framework Coverage

### 1.1 Essential Headline Types Created
- [ ] How-To headline: "How to [Desired Result] Without [Pain Point]"
- [ ] Question headline: "Do You [Common Problem]?"
- [ ] Number/List headline: "[Number] Ways to [Desired Result]"
- [ ] Curiosity headline: "The Secret to [Desired Result] That [Experts] Don't Want You to Know"
- [ ] Testimonial headline: "How [Person] [Achieved Result] in [Time]"
- [ ] Command headline: "Stop [Pain Behavior] and Start [Desired Behavior]"
- [ ] Reason-Why headline: "[Number] Reasons Why [Claim]"

### 1.2 Minimum Set Size
- [ ] At least 10 headline variations written
- [ ] At least 3 different frameworks represented
- [ ] Both emotional and logical angles covered
- [ ] Short (under 7 words) and long (under 15 words) versions
- [ ] At least 2 "wildcard" unconventional headlines

---

## STEP 2: Headline Quality Criteria

### 2.1 Clarity Check
- [ ] Each headline communicates the core benefit clearly
- [ ] A stranger would understand the offer from the headline alone
- [ ] No jargon or insider terms that confuse newcomers
- [ ] Specific enough to attract the right audience
- [ ] Broad enough to not exclude viable prospects

### 2.2 Emotional Trigger Check
- [ ] At least 3 headlines trigger curiosity
- [ ] At least 2 headlines trigger desire/aspiration
- [ ] At least 2 headlines trigger fear of missing out
- [ ] At least 1 headline triggers urgency
- [ ] Headlines evoke strong emotional response (not neutral)

### 2.3 Specificity Check
- [ ] Numbers used where possible (specific > vague)
- [ ] Time frames mentioned where relevant
- [ ] Results quantified where possible
- [ ] Target audience called out in at least 3 headlines
- [ ] Unique mechanism or method referenced in at least 2

---

## STEP 3: Testing Strategy

### 3.1 A/B Test Setup
- [ ] Headlines paired for head-to-head testing
- [ ] Only ONE variable changed between paired headlines
- [ ] Testing platform configured (ads, landing page tool)
- [ ] Minimum traffic per variation: 100-500 impressions
- [ ] Statistical significance criteria defined before testing

### 3.2 Test Priorities
- [ ] Test #1: Best emotional angle (desire vs. fear vs. curiosity)
- [ ] Test #2: Best framework (how-to vs. question vs. number)
- [ ] Test #3: Specificity level (more specific vs. more broad)
- [ ] Test #4: Length (short and punchy vs. detailed)
- [ ] Test #5: Winner vs. new challengers (ongoing)

---

## STEP 4: Platform Adaptation

### 4.1 Ad Headlines
- [ ] Primary text: attention-grabbing, curiosity-driven
- [ ] Under character limits for each platform
- [ ] Works with and without the supporting image/video
- [ ] CTA implied or explicit in the headline

### 4.2 Landing Page Headlines
- [ ] Matches the ad headline (congruence)
- [ ] Expands on the ad promise
- [ ] Sub-headline adds specificity
- [ ] Works above the fold with design elements

### 4.3 Email Subject Lines
- [ ] Subject line versions of top headlines created
- [ ] Personal and conversational tone
- [ ] Under 50 characters for mobile preview
- [ ] Open-loop or curiosity-based preferred

---

## STEP 5: Headline Swipe File

### 5.1 Documentation
- [ ] All tested headlines logged with performance data
- [ ] Winning headlines marked and categorized
- [ ] Losing headlines analyzed for learning
- [ ] Swipe file of 50+ headline formulas maintained
- [ ] Updated monthly with new winners and patterns

---

## Validation
- [ ] Minimum 10 headline variations ready for testing
- [ ] Each headline passes clarity, emotion, and specificity checks
- [ ] Testing plan documented with clear success criteria
- [ ] Top 3 headlines would make YOU click if you were the target
- [ ] Headlines reviewed by someone in the target audience

---

## Scoring
- 26-30/30 = Professional headline testing set
- 20-25/30 = Good set, add more variations or test criteria
- 15-19/30 = Headline set too narrow, diversify approaches
- Below 15 = More headline training needed before testing
