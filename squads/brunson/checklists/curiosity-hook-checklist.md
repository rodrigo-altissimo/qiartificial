# Curiosity Hook Checklist

> Agent: brunson-hooks
> Source: Traffic Secrets & Expert Secrets - Curiosity & Open Loops
> Version: 1.0
> Use: When crafting or evaluating curiosity-based hooks for any content

---

## Pre-Requisites
- [ ] Target audience and their knowledge gaps identified
- [ ] Core topic or offer the hook will lead to defined
- [ ] Desired next action clear (click, watch, read, buy)
- [ ] Platform where hook will appear identified

---

## STEP 1: Information Gap Creation

### 1.1 The Gap
- [ ] Hook reveals ENOUGH to create interest
- [ ] Hook withholds ENOUGH to create a gap
- [ ] Gap is specific (not vague curiosity)
- [ ] Reader believes the answer exists and is reachable

### 1.2 Gap Types
- [ ] "What" gap: "The #1 thing that..." (what is it?)
- [ ] "How" gap: "How she went from X to Y..." (how did she do it?)
- [ ] "Why" gap: "Why most people fail at..." (why does this happen?)
- [ ] "Who" gap: "The person behind the $X empire..." (who is it?)

---

## STEP 2: Curiosity Amplifiers

### 2.1 Specificity
- [ ] Includes specific numbers or details
- [ ] Uses precise timeframes ("in 37 days" vs "quickly")
- [ ] Names specific outcomes ("$47K/month" vs "a lot of money")
- [ ] Details make the claim feel real and verifiable

### 2.2 Contradiction/Counterintuition
- [ ] Challenges a common belief or assumption
- [ ] Presents a surprising or unexpected angle
- [ ] Reader thinks "Wait, that can't be right..." and must find out
- [ ] Contradiction is genuine, not manufactured

### 2.3 Incompleteness
- [ ] Lists tease without completing ("3 steps to X: #1 is...")
- [ ] Stories start but don't finish ("And then she discovered...")
- [ ] Frameworks hint at more ("The first part of the formula is...")
- [ ] Reader MUST engage to get completion

---

## STEP 3: Curiosity Hook Formulas

### 3.1 The "Secret" Formula
- [ ] "The secret [outcome] that [authority] uses..."
- [ ] Secret feels exclusive and insider
- [ ] Implies unfair advantage for those who know

### 3.2 The "Mistake" Formula
- [ ] "The #1 mistake [audience] makes with [topic]..."
- [ ] Reader fears they might be making this mistake
- [ ] Creates urgency to find out and correct

### 3.3 The "Unexpected" Formula
- [ ] "I never expected [surprising thing] when I [action]..."
- [ ] Subverts expectations about the outcome
- [ ] Reader curious about the surprise element

### 3.4 The "Challenge" Formula
- [ ] "Can you [do X] without [common approach]?"
- [ ] Challenges the reader's current method
- [ ] Implies a better way exists

---

## STEP 4: Quality Filters

### 4.1 Ethical Check
- [ ] Hook is truthful (the content delivers on the curiosity)
- [ ] Not pure clickbait (substance matches the tease)
- [ ] Reader will feel satisfied, not tricked, after consuming
- [ ] Curiosity is earned, not manipulated

### 4.2 Relevance Check
- [ ] Hook is relevant to the target audience specifically
- [ ] Connects to a real pain, desire, or question they have
- [ ] Would not work on a random person (it is targeted)
- [ ] Leads naturally to the offer or content behind it

---

## Validation
- [ ] Hook creates genuine "I need to know" feeling
- [ ] Hook tested on 3 people outside the project
- [ ] At least 2 out of 3 said they would click/read/watch
- [ ] Hook delivers on its implicit promise in the content
- [ ] Hook is concise (under 20 words for ads, under 10 for video)

---

## Scoring
- 24-28/28 = Powerful curiosity hook, deploy immediately
- 18-23/28 = Strong hook, refine one element
- 12-17/28 = Curiosity is weak, needs sharper angle
- Below 12 = Scrap and start with a different approach
