# Status System Checklist

> Agent: brunson-movement
> Source: Expert Secrets - Status, Identity & Movement Dynamics
> Version: 1.0
> Use: When designing or auditing a status/progression system for a community or offer

---

## Pre-Requisites
- [ ] Community or membership platform established
- [ ] Value Ladder defined (status often maps to ladder position)
- [ ] Member journey from beginner to advanced mapped
- [ ] Recognition and reward budget/resources allocated

---

## STEP 1: Status Levels Design

### 1.1 Level Structure
- [ ] 3-5 clearly defined status levels created
- [ ] Each level has a distinct name (branded, aspirational)
- [ ] Progression criteria from one level to next are clear
- [ ] Levels feel achievable but meaningful
- [ ] Top level is aspirational (most members want to reach it)

### 1.2 Level Benefits
- [ ] Each level has exclusive benefits or access
- [ ] Benefits increase meaningfully with each level
- [ ] Higher levels get more personal access to the leader
- [ ] Benefits are visible to other members (creates aspiration)

### 1.3 Status Indicators
- [ ] Visual badges, icons, or titles for each level
- [ ] Status visible in community interactions (next to name)
- [ ] Physical rewards for top levels (plaques, rings, jackets)
- [ ] Status is a source of pride and identity

---

## STEP 2: Progression Mechanics

### 2.1 Earning Status
- [ ] Clear criteria for advancing (results, engagement, purchases)
- [ ] Multiple paths to advance (not just spending money)
- [ ] Progress is trackable by the member
- [ ] Advancement feels earned, not bought

### 2.2 Status Maintenance
- [ ] Requirements to maintain current level defined
- [ ] Grace periods for temporary drops in activity
- [ ] Re-qualification process is fair and clear
- [ ] Members understand what keeps them at their level

### 2.3 Celebration of Advancement
- [ ] Public recognition when someone levels up
- [ ] Community celebration rituals for advancement
- [ ] Leader personally acknowledges top-level achievements
- [ ] Advancement stories shared as inspiration

---

## STEP 3: Status & the Value Ladder

### 3.1 Status-Offer Alignment
- [ ] Each status level maps to a position on the Value Ladder
- [ ] Higher status members are naturally presented with higher offers
- [ ] Ascending the ladder increases status (creates buying motivation)
- [ ] Status benefits justify the investment at each tier

### 3.2 Exclusivity
- [ ] Top-tier status has genuine scarcity (limited spots)
- [ ] Exclusivity creates desire among lower tiers
- [ ] Application or qualification process for premium levels
- [ ] "Inner circle" feeling at the highest tier

---

## STEP 4: Social Proof & Aspiration

### 4.1 Status Visibility
- [ ] Success stories highlight members who advanced levels
- [ ] Status leaderboards or showcases visible to all
- [ ] Higher-status members featured in content and events
- [ ] New members can see what is possible (aspiration)

### 4.2 Peer Dynamics
- [ ] Higher-status members mentor lower-status members
- [ ] Cross-level interaction encouraged (not siloed)
- [ ] Status creates healthy competition (not toxicity)
- [ ] Community celebrates collective advancement

---

## Validation
- [ ] All status levels clearly documented with criteria and benefits
- [ ] At least 3 members have progressed through multiple levels
- [ ] Status system increases engagement (measured)
- [ ] Status system drives ascension on the Value Ladder
- [ ] No members report the system as unfair or confusing

---

## Scoring
- 24-28/28 = Compelling status system driving engagement and revenue
- 18-23/28 = Good system, refine benefits or progression mechanics
- 12-17/28 = System exists but is not driving desired behavior
- Below 12 = Status system needs fundamental redesign
