# Seinfeld Email Template Quality Checklist

> Source: DotCom Secrets, Chapter 5 - Daily Seinfeld Emails
> Version: 1.0
> Use: When writing or reviewing ongoing daily/weekly Seinfeld-style emails

---

## Pre-Requisites
- [ ] Soap Opera Sequence complete (Seinfeld emails follow it)
- [ ] Attractive Character profile defined
- [ ] Story inventory of 20+ personal stories/observations
- [ ] Core offer(s) ready for soft-pitch integration

---

## STEP 1: Email Content Quality

### 1.1 Story/Entertainment Element
- [ ] Email opens with a story, observation, or entertaining hook
- [ ] Story is about daily life, NOT directly about the product
- [ ] Reader is entertained first, educated second
- [ ] Personal voice shines through (feels like a friend's email)
- [ ] Story is specific (real event, real observation, real person)

### 1.2 Lesson Connection
- [ ] Daily story naturally connects to a business/life lesson
- [ ] Connection feels organic (not forced or preachy)
- [ ] Lesson relates to your core teaching themes
- [ ] Reader gets genuine insight from the email
- [ ] "Aha" moment embedded within the narrative

### 1.3 CTA Integration
- [ ] Every email includes a CTA (even if soft)
- [ ] CTA connects to current offer or content
- [ ] CTA feels like a natural extension of the lesson
- [ ] Link to offer/page included in PS or within body
- [ ] NOT every email is a hard sell (mix: 80% value, 20% direct pitch)

---

## STEP 2: Email Structure

### 2.1 Subject Lines
- [ ] Subject line is curiosity-driven (not descriptive)
- [ ] Does NOT reveal the lesson or story (opens a loop)
- [ ] Under 50 characters for mobile optimization
- [ ] Personal and conversational tone
- [ ] Avoids spam trigger words (free, buy now, limited time)

### 2.2 Body Format
- [ ] Short paragraphs (1-3 sentences each)
- [ ] Total length: 200-500 words (quick read)
- [ ] Conversational tone throughout
- [ ] One main idea per email (not multiple topics)
- [ ] Easy to scan and read on mobile

### 2.3 Signature Elements
- [ ] Consistent sign-off that builds brand recognition
- [ ] PS line used for secondary CTA or teaser
- [ ] Reply invitation encouraged (builds deliverability)
- [ ] Personality consistent email to email

---

## STEP 3: Topic Variety

### 3.1 Story Sources
- [ ] Personal daily life observations (coffee shop, gym, kids)
- [ ] Customer interactions and questions
- [ ] Current events tied to your niche
- [ ] Lessons from books, podcasts, or conversations
- [ ] Behind-the-scenes of your business

### 3.2 Topic Rotation
- [ ] Not all emails about the same topic
- [ ] Mix: entertaining, educational, inspiring, controversial
- [ ] At least 1 email per week is pure entertainment
- [ ] At least 1 email per week is pure value/teaching
- [ ] At least 1 email per week directly pitches an offer

### 3.3 Content Calendar
- [ ] 30 days of email topics brainstormed in advance
- [ ] Topics mapped to Attractive Character pillars
- [ ] Seasonal and timely topics integrated
- [ ] Evergreen emails marked for potential reuse
- [ ] Backup topics available if current events shift plans

---

## STEP 4: Engagement Metrics

### 4.1 Performance Tracking
- [ ] Open rate tracked (target: 20%+ for warm list)
- [ ] Click rate tracked (target: 2%+ per email)
- [ ] Unsubscribe rate monitored (acceptable: under 0.5% per email)
- [ ] Reply rate noted (higher = better engagement)
- [ ] Best-performing emails analyzed for patterns

### 4.2 Optimization
- [ ] Subject lines A/B tested on large sends
- [ ] Send time tested (morning vs. afternoon vs. evening)
- [ ] Day of week tested for optimal engagement
- [ ] Long vs. short email length tested
- [ ] Winners inform future email strategy

---

## STEP 5: Consistency and Sustainability

### 5.1 Publishing Cadence
- [ ] Frequency chosen: daily, 3x/week, or weekly (and stuck to)
- [ ] Batching system: write 5-7 emails in one session
- [ ] Buffer: always 3-5 emails written ahead of schedule
- [ ] System for capturing story ideas in real-time (notes app)
- [ ] If you miss a day, email about why (turns lapse into content)

### 5.2 Long-Term Health
- [ ] List grows faster than it shrinks (net positive monthly)
- [ ] Revenue per email tracked and trending upward
- [ ] Audience engagement increasing or stable over time
- [ ] Reader feedback and replies inform content strategy
- [ ] Email is the #1 revenue channel (or close to it)

---

## Validation
- [ ] Minimum 10 Seinfeld emails written and reviewed
- [ ] Each email has story + lesson + CTA
- [ ] Emails feel like they come from a real person (not a brand)
- [ ] Test readers say they'd look forward to receiving these
- [ ] Automation or scheduling system tested and working

---

## Scoring
- 30-34/34 = Engaging, revenue-generating Seinfeld email system
- 24-29/34 = Strong emails, improve consistency or variety
- 18-23/34 = Emails need more personality or better CTAs
- Below 18 = Study Seinfeld email concept and rewrite
