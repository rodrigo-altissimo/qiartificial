# Story Inventory Completeness Checklist

> Source: Expert Secrets + DotCom Secrets - Story-Based Selling
> Version: 1.0
> Use: When building or auditing your library of stories for marketing and sales

---

## Pre-Requisites
- [ ] Attractive Character profile defined
- [ ] Key false beliefs mapped (Vehicle, Internal, External)
- [ ] Core offer(s) defined
- [ ] Understanding of the role stories play in selling

---

## STEP 1: Origin Stories

### 1.1 Personal Origin
- [ ] Your origin story written (how you started in this field)
- [ ] Story includes struggle and failure (not just success)
- [ ] Turning point / epiphany moment clearly defined
- [ ] Story shows before/after transformation
- [ ] Story is polished and can be told in 3-5 minutes

### 1.2 Business Origin
- [ ] How the business/product was created (discovery story)
- [ ] Problem that inspired the creation
- [ ] First customer or success story
- [ ] Why you chose THIS approach over alternatives
- [ ] Business origin reinforces credibility and mission

---

## STEP 2: Epiphany Bridge Stories

### 2.1 Vehicle Belief Stories (Minimum 3)
- [ ] Story 1: How YOU realized the vehicle works
- [ ] Story 2: How a STUDENT/CLIENT realized it works
- [ ] Story 3: How an OUTSIDER/SKEPTIC was convinced
- [ ] Each story breaks a different vehicle false belief
- [ ] Stories feature diverse characters for broad appeal

### 2.2 Internal Belief Stories (Minimum 3)
- [ ] Story 1: Overcoming self-doubt ("I'm not smart enough")
- [ ] Story 2: Overcoming skill gap ("I don't have the skills")
- [ ] Story 3: Overcoming resource limitation ("I don't have time/money")
- [ ] Each story features someone the audience relates to
- [ ] Stories prove that ordinary people can achieve results

### 2.3 External Belief Stories (Minimum 3)
- [ ] Story 1: Succeeding despite market conditions
- [ ] Story 2: Succeeding despite timing concerns
- [ ] Story 3: Succeeding despite competition or saturation
- [ ] Stories reframe external factors as opportunities
- [ ] Stories create urgency for acting NOW

---

## STEP 3: Proof Stories

### 3.1 Case Studies
- [ ] Minimum 5 detailed case studies written
- [ ] Each includes: challenge, solution, specific results
- [ ] Results are quantified where possible
- [ ] Permission obtained to share each case study
- [ ] Case studies represent diverse audience segments

### 3.2 Testimonial Stories
- [ ] 10+ testimonials collected (video and text)
- [ ] Testimonials organized by objection they address
- [ ] Each testimonial tells a mini-story (not just "great product")
- [ ] Testimonials feature real names and identifiers
- [ ] Fresh testimonials collected quarterly

### 3.3 Data Stories
- [ ] Key statistics and data points that support your approach
- [ ] Industry research that validates your methodology
- [ ] Your own data from implementations and results
- [ ] Comparisons that favor your approach vs. alternatives
- [ ] Data presented in story format (not just raw numbers)

---

## STEP 4: Teaching Stories (Parables)

### 4.1 Concept Illustration Stories
- [ ] Minimum 5 teaching parables collected
- [ ] Each parable teaches a core principle through narrative
- [ ] Parables use everyday analogies (cooking, sports, driving)
- [ ] Parables are memorable and retellable
- [ ] Parables can be used across formats (email, stage, video)

### 4.2 Cautionary Tales
- [ ] Stories of what happens when people DON'T follow the method
- [ ] Stories of what happens when people follow OLD approaches
- [ ] Pain of inaction made vivid through narrative
- [ ] Cautionary tales are real (or clearly marked as illustrative)
- [ ] Tales motivate action without being fear-mongering

---

## STEP 5: Content and Connection Stories

### 5.1 Daily Life Stories (Seinfeld-Style)
- [ ] 20+ daily life observations collected
- [ ] Each connects to a business/life principle
- [ ] Stories are entertaining even without the lesson
- [ ] Stories reveal your personality and humanity
- [ ] Stories can be used for email, social, and content

### 5.2 Vulnerability Stories
- [ ] 3-5 stories of personal failure or mistakes
- [ ] Stories show what you learned from each failure
- [ ] Stories build trust through authenticity
- [ ] Vulnerability is genuine, not performed
- [ ] Stories are shared strategically (not oversharing)

---

## STEP 6: Story Organization

### 6.1 Story Library System
- [ ] All stories documented in one accessible location
- [ ] Stories tagged by: type, belief addressed, emotion, use case
- [ ] Quick-reference summary for each story (1-2 sentences)
- [ ] Full versions written out for copy and scripts
- [ ] Short versions available for social media and ads

### 6.2 Story Calendar
- [ ] Stories mapped to content calendar
- [ ] Rotation ensures variety (same story not overused)
- [ ] New stories added monthly from experiences and clients
- [ ] Stories tested and rated by audience response
- [ ] Best performers flagged for repeated use

---

## Validation
- [ ] Minimum 30 stories in inventory across all categories
- [ ] Every false belief has at least 2 stories addressing it
- [ ] Stories tested: audiences respond emotionally and take action
- [ ] Story library is accessible and organized for quick retrieval
- [ ] Story collection is an ongoing habit (not a one-time activity)

---

## Scoring
- 32-36/36 = Comprehensive, well-organized story inventory
- 26-31/36 = Strong inventory, fill gaps and organize better
- 20-25/36 = Significant gaps in story coverage
- Below 20 = Story collection needs to be a priority
