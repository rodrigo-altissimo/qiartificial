# Manifesto Checklist

> Agent: brunson-movement
> Source: Expert Secrets - Mass Movement & Manifesto Creation
> Version: 1.0
> Use: When writing or auditing a movement manifesto for a brand or cause

---

## Pre-Requisites
- [ ] Attractive Character / leader established
- [ ] Core beliefs and values documented (minimum 3-5)
- [ ] Target audience's frustrations with the status quo identified
- [ ] New opportunity or vehicle defined
- [ ] Vision for the future clearly articulated

---

## STEP 1: The Problem Declaration

### 1.1 The Enemy
- [ ] Clear enemy or injustice identified (NOT a person, but a system/belief)
- [ ] Enemy is something the audience already resents
- [ ] Specific examples of how the enemy harms the audience
- [ ] Emotional connection to the frustration is established

### 1.2 The Broken Promise
- [ ] Old way/status quo described and its failures named
- [ ] "They told you [X], but the truth is [Y]" pattern used
- [ ] Reader feels validated ("Finally, someone sees what I see")
- [ ] Frustration channeled into motivation for change

---

## STEP 2: The Vision

### 2.1 The New World
- [ ] Clear picture of what the world looks like AFTER the change
- [ ] Vision is specific and vivid (not vague platitudes)
- [ ] Reader can see themselves in this new world
- [ ] Vision is aspirational but believable

### 2.2 The Path
- [ ] New opportunity or vehicle introduced as the way forward
- [ ] Path is presented as different from what has failed before
- [ ] Clear distinction: "This is not another [old thing], this is..."
- [ ] Path feels achievable for the reader

---

## STEP 3: The Beliefs

### 3.1 Core Belief Statements
- [ ] 5-10 "We believe..." statements written
- [ ] Each belief is specific and polarizing (not safe generalities)
- [ ] Beliefs attract the right people and repel the wrong ones
- [ ] Beliefs are ordered from most relatable to most bold

### 3.2 The Line in the Sand
- [ ] Clear declaration of what you stand FOR
- [ ] Clear declaration of what you stand AGAINST
- [ ] Reader forced to choose a side
- [ ] Choosing "your side" feels empowering and exciting

---

## STEP 4: The Call to Action

### 4.1 The Invitation
- [ ] Reader explicitly invited to join the movement
- [ ] Joining feels like an identity shift ("You are now one of us")
- [ ] First step is clear and simple
- [ ] No barrier to entry for the initial commitment

### 4.2 The Commitment
- [ ] Reader asked to commit (share, sign, join, act)
- [ ] Commitment is public or social (increases follow-through)
- [ ] Commitment connects to the bigger mission
- [ ] Reward for commitment is immediate (access, belonging, status)

---

## STEP 5: Manifesto Quality

### 5.1 Tone & Voice
- [ ] Written with passion and conviction (not corporate)
- [ ] Reads like a speech, not a document
- [ ] Uses "we" language (inclusive, collective)
- [ ] Builds emotional momentum from start to finish

### 5.2 Length & Format
- [ ] Between 500-2000 words (powerful but not exhausting)
- [ ] Can be read aloud in under 10 minutes
- [ ] Formatted for impact (short paragraphs, bold statements)
- [ ] Works as text, audio, or video

---

## Validation
- [ ] Manifesto read aloud and it creates emotional response
- [ ] 3 people in target audience said it resonated deeply
- [ ] At least 1 person said "I want to share this"
- [ ] Manifesto is consistent with all other brand messaging
- [ ] Call to action has a specific, trackable next step

---

## Scoring
- 26-30/30 = Powerful manifesto, ready to publish
- 20-25/30 = Strong manifesto, refine tone or clarity
- 14-19/30 = Has the right ideas but lacks emotional power
- Below 14 = Rewrite with deeper conviction and specificity
