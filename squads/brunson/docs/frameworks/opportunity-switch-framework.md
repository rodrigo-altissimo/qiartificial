# Opportunity Switch Framework

> Source: Expert Secrets, Chapter 4 — Russell Brunson
> Why new opportunities beat improvements every time

---

## The Core Insight

"People don't want to IMPROVE what they're already doing. They want a NEW OPPORTUNITY that gives them fresh hope."

There are two ways to present your offer:
1. **Improvement Offer** (weak): "Let me help you do what you're already doing, but better"
2. **New Opportunity** (powerful): "Stop doing that. I found something completely new that works better"

---

## Why Switch > Stack

### The Status Problem
Every purchase decision is filtered through: "Will this increase or decrease my perceived status?"

- If someone buys an IMPROVEMENT, they're admitting their current approach is failing (status DECREASE)
- If someone buys a NEW OPPORTUNITY, they're a pioneer discovering something new (status INCREASE)

### Improvement Offer (Opportunity Stack)
- "Keep doing what you're doing + add this"
- Implies current failure
- Creates resistance ("I've already tried everything")
- Low excitement, high skepticism
- Example: "Better diet plan" (implies their current diet sucks)

### New Opportunity (Opportunity Switch)
- "Forget everything you know. This is completely different."
- Preserves status ("It's not that I failed — I just didn't have the right vehicle")
- Creates excitement ("This could be the answer")
- High hope, lower skepticism
- Example: "Intermittent fasting" (completely new approach)

---

## How to Frame Your Offer as a New Opportunity

### Step 1: Identify What They're Currently Doing
What vehicle are they using now? What methods have they tried?

### Step 2: Explain Why the Old Way No Longer Works
"The reason [old method] stopped working is because [legitimate reason]."

### Step 3: Introduce the New Vehicle
"What I discovered is a completely different approach called [name]."

### Step 4: Show Why It's Categorically Different
Not 10% better — fundamentally different in how it works.

### Step 5: Share the Epiphany Bridge
Your story of discovering this new opportunity.

## Examples

| Market | Improvement (Weak) | New Opportunity (Strong) |
|--------|-------------------|--------------------------|
| Weight Loss | "Better diet and exercise plan" | "Ketogenic metabolism reset" |
| Marketing | "Better Facebook ads" | "Funnel-based customer journey" |
| Finance | "Better budgeting app" | "Cash flow system that pays you first" |
| Productivity | "Better to-do list" | "Energy management (not time management)" |
| Business | "Better sales techniques" | "Attraction-based selling through funnels" |

---

## Key Principles

1. **New > Better** — Always frame as new, never as improved
2. **Status preservation** — Let them save face about past failures
3. **Give it a NAME** — New opportunities need unique names
4. **Tell the discovery story** — Use Epiphany Bridge
5. **Create contrast** — Show old way vs. new way side by side
