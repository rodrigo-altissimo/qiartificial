# Attractive Character Framework

> Source: DotCom Secrets, Chapters 4-5 — Russell Brunson
> Building the persona that your audience connects with and buys from

---

## The Concept

"People don't buy from businesses. They buy from PEOPLE. Your Attractive Character (AC) is the personality your audience builds a relationship with."

The AC is not about being fake — it's about intentionally sharing specific parts of who you are to create connection, trust, and loyalty.

---

## The 4 Elements of an Attractive Character

### 1. Backstory
**Purpose**: Creates relatability — "They're just like me"

- Your origin story (before the transformation)
- Struggles your audience relates to
- The journey that led to your expertise
- Key: Be vulnerable about your PAST, confident about your PRESENT

### 2. Parables
**Purpose**: Teaching stories that illustrate principles

- Everyday situations that teach business/life lessons
- Analogies that make complex ideas simple
- Stories you tell over and over (your "greatest hits")
- Key: Every parable should tie back to a core belief

### 3. Character Flaws
**Purpose**: Vulnerability creates trust

- Imperfections that make you human
- Past mistakes you openly discuss
- Ongoing struggles (not weaknesses in your expertise)
- Key: Flaws make you RELATABLE, not LESS credible

### 4. Polarity
**Purpose**: Strong opinions that attract fans and repel non-fits

- Controversial positions in your industry
- Things you're willing to say that others won't
- Clear stances that create "us vs. them"
- Key: If no one disagrees with you, no one STRONGLY agrees either

## The 4 Identity Types

### The Leader
- "Follow me, I've been where you are and I'll show you the way"
- Already achieved results, now leads others
- Tone: Authoritative but empathetic
- Examples: Russell Brunson, Tony Robbins

### The Adventurer / Crusader
- "Join me on this quest — we'll discover it together"
- On an active journey, sharing in real-time
- Tone: Exciting, raw, experiential
- Examples: Gary Vaynerchuk, Casey Neistat

### The Reporter / Evangelist
- "I found something amazing and I have to share it with you"
- Curates and shares knowledge from experts
- Tone: Curious, investigative, connecting dots
- Examples: Tim Ferriss, Oprah

### The Reluctant Hero
- "I didn't want the spotlight, but I can't keep this to myself"
- Pushed into leadership by results or duty
- Tone: Humble, authentic, understated
- Examples: Many info-product creators, technical experts

## Building Your AC

### Step 1: Choose Your Identity Type
Which feels most natural? You can blend, but lead with one.

### Step 2: Mine Your Backstory
What's your origin story? Write the before/transformation/after.

### Step 3: Catalog Your Parables
List 10+ stories from your life that teach principles.

### Step 4: Embrace Your Flaws
What makes you imperfect? What have you failed at?

### Step 5: Define Your Polarity
What do you believe that's controversial? What hill will you die on?

---

## AC Communication Rules

1. **First person always** — "I" not "we" or "the company"
2. **Share daily life** — Mundane moments with business lessons
3. **Be consistent** — Same voice across all channels
4. **Evolve publicly** — Let the audience grow with you
5. **Take stands** — Don't be afraid to lose some people

---

## Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Fix |
|---------|-----|
| Being too professional / corporate | Show personality, be human |
| No vulnerability | Share past struggles and failures |
| No polarity | Take a stand on something controversial |
| Inconsistent voice | Create a voice guide and stick to it |
| Hiding behind the brand | Be the face, use first person |
