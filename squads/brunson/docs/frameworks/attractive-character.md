# Attractive Character Framework

## Backstory + Flaws + Polarity + Parables

---

## Overview

The Attractive Character (AC) is Russell Brunson's framework for building a magnetic personal brand that people want to follow, listen to, and buy from. People buy from PEOPLE, not from companies. The AC is the personality behind your brand -- the voice in your emails, the face on your videos, the storyteller in your webinars. It is built on four core elements (Backstory, Parables, Character Flaws, Polarity) and expressed through specific identity types and storylines. The AC is not about being fake -- it is about strategically sharing the authentic parts of yourself that create connection and trust.

---

## Core Principles

1. **People Buy From People** -- Brands without personality are forgettable
2. **Authenticity With Strategy** -- Share real parts of yourself, but choose which parts strategically
3. **Flaws Create Connection** -- Perfect people are unrelatable; vulnerability builds trust
4. **Polarity Attracts** -- Taking strong positions repels some but magnetically attracts your people
5. **Stories Are Currency** -- Your life experiences are marketing assets when framed correctly
6. **Consistency Creates Trust** -- Show up as the same character across all channels

---

## The Four Elements

### Element 1: BACKSTORY (Origin Story)

Your journey from where you started to where you are now.

**Components:**
- **Moment of crisis or pain** that started your journey
- **Decision to change** -- the turning point
- **Obstacles overcome** -- the struggle that makes you relatable
- **Transformation achieved** -- proof that the journey works

**Why it works:** Your backstory is your Epiphany Bridge. It lets the audience see themselves in your starting point and believe they can reach your current position.

### Element 2: PARABLES (Teaching Stories)

Stories from your life that teach lessons related to your expertise.

**Sources for parables:**
- Personal experiences and failures
- Client success stories and transformations
- Observations from daily life
- Historical or cultural analogies
- Metaphors that simplify complex ideas

**Why it works:** Parables let you teach without preaching. A story about your kid's lemonade stand can teach more about pricing psychology than a textbook.

### Element 3: CHARACTER FLAWS

Imperfections that make you human and relatable.

**Types of productive flaws:**
- Admitting past mistakes openly
- Sharing ongoing struggles (imposter syndrome, procrastination)
- Being transparent about failures
- Showing the messy behind-the-scenes

**Why it works:** Perfection creates distance. Flaws create intimacy. When you share a flaw, the audience thinks "they are just like me" -- and that is when trust is born.

### Element 4: POLARITY

Taking strong, definitive positions that some will love and others will hate.

**Polarity principles:**
- Stand FOR something clearly
- Stand AGAINST something clearly
- Never be lukewarm or wishy-washy
- Accept that repelling some people is necessary to magnetically attract others
- Stay in the "Prolific Zone" -- strong opinions, but not offensive or extreme

**Why it works:** Neutral brands are invisible. Polarizing brands create passionate followers. You do not need everyone to like you -- you need YOUR people to love you.

## Identity Types

The AC typically fits one of four archetypes:

| Type | Description | Example |
|------|-------------|---------|
| **The Leader** | Leads from the front; "follow me" | Russell Brunson, Tony Robbins |
| **The Adventurer** | Explores and reports back; "look what I found" | Tim Ferriss, Bear Grylls |
| **The Reporter** | Interviews and curates; "here is what I learned" | Joe Rogan, Oprah |
| **The Reluctant Hero** | Did not want the spotlight but felt compelled | Many founders with imposter syndrome |

You can embody one or combine elements of multiple types.

---

## AC Storyline Types

Six recurring story formats for your content:

1. **Loss and Redemption** -- How you fell and rose again
2. **Us vs. Them** -- Your tribe vs. the establishment / old way
3. **Before and After** -- Transformation story (yours or a client's)
4. **Amazing Discovery** -- "I just found something incredible..."
5. **Secret Telling** -- "Nobody talks about this, but..."
6. **Third Person Testimonial** -- Someone else's success with your method

---

## Step-by-Step Application

1. **Write your Origin Story** using the Epiphany Bridge 8-step structure
2. **List 20 life parables** -- Stories from your life that connect to your expertise
3. **Identify 3-5 character flaws** you are comfortable sharing publicly
4. **Define your polarity positions** -- What do you stand FOR and AGAINST?
5. **Choose your identity type** -- Leader, Adventurer, Reporter, or Reluctant Hero
6. **Create a content calendar** rotating through the 6 storyline types
7. **Be consistent** -- Use the same AC voice in emails, videos, social, webinars

## Common Mistakes

1. **Being too polished** -- Perfect, corporate-feeling content that lacks personality
2. **No polarity** -- Trying to please everyone results in connecting with no one
3. **Inconsistent character** -- Different personality on different platforms confuses the audience
4. **All teaching, no stories** -- Information without narrative is forgettable
5. **Faking flaws** -- Inauthentic vulnerability is worse than no vulnerability
6. **Hiding behind a brand** -- Using a company name instead of a personal voice
